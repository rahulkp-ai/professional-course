# Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving

## 1. Key Elements of Critical Thinking

Critical thinking involves carefully analyzing information, evaluating evidence, drawing logical conclusions, communicating reasoning, and reflecting on one's own thinking.

### 1.1 Analysis

**Analysis** means breaking complex information into smaller components to understand how the parts relate to each other and to the whole.

**Purpose:**

- Identify patterns.
- Understand causes and relationships.
- Discover underlying issues.
- Approach problems logically and step by step.

> **Key idea:** Break a complex problem into smaller, manageable parts.

### 1.2 Evaluation

**Evaluation** involves determining whether information is valid, relevant, reliable, and supported by sufficient evidence.

It requires examining:

- Sources of information.
- Quality of evidence.
- Arguments and reasoning.
- Relevance of the information.

**Purpose:**

- Filter out misinformation.
- Make decisions based on reliable evidence.
- Improve confidence in decision-making.

> **Key idea:** Check whether the information and evidence can actually be trusted.

### 1.3 Inference

**Inference** means drawing logical conclusions from available evidence, observations, and existing knowledge.

It involves:

- Interpreting information that is not directly stated.
- Connecting evidence to possible conclusions.
- Making reasonable predictions.
- Anticipating potential outcomes.

> **Key idea:** Use available evidence to determine what is most likely to be true or happen next.

### 1.4 Explanation

**Explanation** is the ability to clearly communicate findings, reasoning, and conclusions.

A strong explanation should:

- Present ideas in a logical structure.
- Clearly communicate the reasoning process.
- Provide evidence for conclusions.
- Make the thought process understandable to others.

**Purpose:**

- Improve clarity.
- Help others understand the reasoning.
- Build trust in decisions and conclusions.

> **Key idea:** Clearly explain not only _what_ you concluded, but also _why_.

### 1.5 Reflection

**Reflection** means examining your own thinking, assumptions, biases, and decisions.

It involves considering:

- Personal biases.
- Existing assumptions.
- Past experiences that may influence judgment.
- Alternative perspectives.

**Purpose:**

- Promote objective thinking.
- Identify weaknesses in reasoning.
- Learn from mistakes.
- Encourage continuous improvement.

> **Key idea:** Step back and question your own thinking.

---

## 2. Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving

Problem-solving through critical thinking is a **deliberate, structured, and step-by-step process**.

Each stage builds on the previous stage:

1. **Identify the problem clearly**
2. **Analyze the available information**
3. **Generate possible solutions**
4. **Evaluate the possible solutions**
5. **Choose the most effective course of action**

### Problem-Solving Flow

```text
Identify Problem
       ↓
    Analyze
       ↓
Generate Solutions
       ↓
    Evaluate
       ↓
Choose Best Solution
       ↓
    Take Action
       ↓
    Reflect
```

| Element         | Meaning                              | Main Purpose                             |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Analysis**    | Break information into smaller parts | Understand relationships and causes      |
| **Evaluation**  | Assess validity and relevance        | Determine whether evidence is reliable   |
| **Inference**   | Draw conclusions from evidence       | Make logical predictions                 |
| **Explanation** | Communicate reasoning clearly        | Justify conclusions                      |
| **Reflection**  | Examine your own thinking            | Reduce bias and improve future decisions |
