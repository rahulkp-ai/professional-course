# Problem Solving Using Critical Thinking

## Step 3: Identify the Root Cause

After gathering and analyzing information, the next step is to **identify the root cause** of the problem.

The goal is to move beyond symptoms and determine **why the problem is happening in the first place**.

---

## 1. Mia's Journey

Mia meets with her assistant **Lily** and developer **Alex** to investigate the problem further.

The team has already established:

> **Incomplete requirements are causing rework and project delays.**

However, Mia asks an important question:

> **Why were the requirements incomplete in the first place?**

The team considers several possibilities:

- Communication gaps.
- Unclear responsibilities.
- Confusion in task assignment.
- Overlapping roles.
- Other process-related issues.

Rather than immediately choosing one explanation, Mia encourages the team to investigate further.

> **Don't jump to conclusions. Dig deeper and identify the actual cause.**

To do this, Mia uses the **5 Whys technique**.

---

# 2. The 5 Whys Technique

The **5 Whys** is a root-cause analysis technique that involves repeatedly asking **"Why?"** to uncover the underlying cause of a problem.

Each question moves deeper into the chain of causes.

### Purpose

The technique helps:

- Move beyond surface-level symptoms.
- Identify underlying causes.
- Avoid quick and superficial fixes.
- Encourage deeper critical thinking.
- Understand how one problem leads to another.

> **Key idea:** Keep asking "Why?" until you reach a cause that explains the problem at a deeper, systemic level.

---

# 3. Mia's 5 Whys Analysis

### Problem

> **Incomplete requirements are causing rework and project delays.**

### Why 1: Why are the requirements incomplete?

**Because the development team did not receive detailed specifications from the business analysts.**

↓

### Why 2: Why didn't the business analysts provide detailed specifications?

**Because they did not fully understand the client's needs during the initial requirements-gathering process.**

↓

### Why 3: Why didn't they fully understand the client's needs?

**Because there was insufficient communication between the business analysts and the client during the discovery phase.**

↓

### Why 4: Why was communication insufficient?

**Because the initial project meetings were rushed and there was not enough time for detailed discussions and clarification of client expectations.**

↓

### Why 5: Why were the initial meetings rushed?

**Because the project schedule was too aggressive.**

The team moved into execution without allocating sufficient time for:

- Requirements gathering.
- Client discussions.
- Requirement clarification.
- Requirement analysis.

---

# 4. Root Cause Chain

The complete chain can be represented as:

```text id="2u3x1r"
Aggressive Project Schedule
          ↓
Insufficient Time for Requirements Gathering
          ↓
Rushed Client Meetings
          ↓
Poor Communication / Limited Clarification
          ↓
Business Analysts Don't Fully Understand Client Needs
          ↓
Incomplete Requirements
          ↓
Repeated Rework
          ↓
Development Delays
          ↓
Testing Delays
          ↓
Overall Project Delay
```

---

# 5. Root Cause Identified

The original visible problem was:

> **Testing is taking too long.**

Further analysis showed:

> **Incomplete requirements are causing repeated changes and rework.**

The 5 Whys investigation went deeper and identified the systemic root cause:

> **The project schedule was too aggressive, leaving insufficient time for thorough requirements gathering, client communication, and analysis.**

Therefore, the problem is not simply a testing or development issue.

It is fundamentally connected to **how the project was planned and initiated**.

---

# 6. Symptom → Cause → Root Cause

| Level                | Finding                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Symptom**          | Testing delays                                                                                 |
| **Immediate Cause**  | Repeated development changes and rework                                                        |
| **Underlying Cause** | Incomplete requirements                                                                        |
| **Deeper Cause**     | Business analysts did not fully understand client needs                                        |
| **Root Cause**       | Aggressive project scheduling caused insufficient time for requirements gathering and analysis |

This demonstrates why solving only the visible symptom would be ineffective.

---

# 7. Why Root-Cause Analysis Matters

Identifying the root cause helps organizations create **lasting solutions** rather than temporary fixes.

### Without Root-Cause Analysis

```text
Testing Delay
     ↓
Try to Speed Up Testing
     ↓
Temporary Improvement
     ↓
Same Requirements Problems
     ↓
More Rework
     ↓
Future Delays
```

### With Root-Cause Analysis

```text
Testing Delay
     ↓
Investigate Causes
     ↓
Identify Incomplete Requirements
     ↓
Apply 5 Whys
     ↓
Identify Aggressive Scheduling
     ↓
Improve Project Planning
     ↓
Better Requirements
     ↓
Less Rework
     ↓
Fewer Delays
```

---

# 8. Important Critical-Thinking Principle

The team initially considered several explanations, including task assignment and communication problems.

However, they did not immediately assume one was correct.

This demonstrates an important principle:

> **A hypothesis is not a conclusion. Investigate it using evidence.**

Good critical thinking requires:

1. Considering multiple possible causes.
2. Asking questions.
3. Examining evidence.
4. Testing assumptions.
5. Identifying the most plausible root cause.

---

# 9. 5 Whys Template

The technique can be reused for other problems:

```text
Problem:
Why?
→ ______________________________

Why?
→ ______________________________

Why?
→ ______________________________

Why?
→ ______________________________

Why?
→ ______________________________

Root Cause:
→ ______________________________
```

### Important Note

The technique is called **5 Whys**, but exactly five questions are not mandatory in every situation.

The goal is to continue asking **why** until the underlying cause is sufficiently understood.

---

# 10. Root-Cause Analysis Checklist

- [ ] Have we identified the visible problem?
- [ ] Have we separated symptoms from causes?
- [ ] Have we considered alternative explanations?
- [ ] Have we avoided jumping to conclusions?
- [ ] Have we repeatedly asked "Why?"
- [ ] Is each answer supported by evidence?
- [ ] Have we reached an underlying cause?
- [ ] Does the identified root cause explain the original problem?
- [ ] Would addressing the root cause prevent the problem from recurring?

---

# 11. Key Takeaways

1. **Do not stop at the first apparent cause.**
2. Use the **5 Whys** to investigate deeper.
3. Distinguish between **symptoms, immediate causes, and root causes**.
4. Avoid making assumptions without evidence.
5. Root-cause analysis helps prevent **temporary fixes**.
6. Problems may originate from **systemic processes**, not individual performance.
7. In Mia's case, the deeper issue was **aggressive project scheduling**.
8. Better project planning can provide sufficient time for requirements gathering and analysis.

## Core Principle

> **Don't ask only "What went wrong?" Ask "Why did it happen?" repeatedly until you understand the underlying system that produced the problem.**
