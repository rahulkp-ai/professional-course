# Problem Solving Using Critical Thinking

## Step 1: Define the Problem Clearly

The **first step in critical-thinking-based problem solving is to define the problem clearly**.

A poorly defined problem can lead to solving the wrong issue, wasting time, resources, and effort.

---

## 1. Mia's Journey: Identifying the Real Problem

Mia calls a team meeting to understand why the project is delayed.

### Initial Observation

Alex observes:

> The testing phase is taking too long, causing the project to fall behind schedule.

This is a valid observation, but Mia investigates further.

### Root Cause Discovery

Mia discovers that the **testing delay is only a symptom**, not the root cause.

The actual issue is:

> **The project received incomplete or unclear requirements at the beginning.**

This resulted in:

```text
Incomplete / Unclear Requirements
            ↓
      Repeated Changes
            ↓
          Rework
            ↓
 Development Delays
            ↓
    Testing Delays
            ↓
   Project Delay
```

Therefore, simply trying to make testing faster would not solve the underlying problem.

### Actual Problem

**Ensure that requirements are complete, accurate, and clearly understood at the beginning of the project.**

Fixing the root cause can prevent similar downstream delays in the future.

---

# 2. Symptom vs. Root Cause

One of the most important aspects of problem solving is distinguishing between a **symptom** and the **root cause**.

| Concept        | Meaning                                 | Example                                 |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Symptom**    | Visible effect of a problem             | Testing is taking too long              |
| **Root Cause** | Fundamental reason the problem occurred | Requirements were incomplete or unclear |

### Key Principle

> **Do not immediately solve what you see. Investigate why it is happening.**

A solution that addresses only the symptom may provide temporary improvement while the underlying problem remains.

---

# 3. Why Clearly Defining the Problem Matters

## 3.1 Sets the Right Direction

A clear problem definition creates a **shared understanding** among team members.

It helps:

- Align everyone toward the same objective.
- Prevent teams from working on unrelated issues.
- Focus efforts on meaningful solutions.
- Avoid conflicting interpretations of the problem.

```text
Clear Problem Definition
          ↓
   Shared Understanding
          ↓
      Team Alignment
          ↓
 Focused Problem Solving
```

---

## 3.2 Saves Time and Resources

A well-defined problem allows teams to focus their effort where it matters.

It helps reduce:

- Unnecessary work.
- Incorrect solutions.
- Rework.
- Wasted resources.
- Delays caused by unclear objectives.

> **Better problem definition → fewer missteps → less wasted effort.**

---

## 3.3 Reveals the Root Cause

A vague problem statement often causes people to treat symptoms rather than causes.

Clearly defining the problem encourages deeper investigation by asking:

- What is actually happening?
- Why is it happening?
- What caused it?
- Is the observed issue the real problem?
- What evidence supports our assumption?

This leads to **root-cause analysis** and more sustainable solutions.

---

## 3.4 Improves Communication

A clearly defined problem makes communication easier between:

- Team members
- Managers
- Stakeholders
- Developers
- Customers
- Other departments

When everyone understands the problem in the same way, it:

- Reduces confusion.
- Aligns expectations.
- Improves collaboration.
- Builds trust.
- Supports better decision-making.

---

## 3.5 Creates Measurable Success Criteria

A clear problem definition provides a foundation for measuring whether a solution actually works.

It allows teams to establish:

- Specific goals.
- Performance metrics.
- Milestones.
- Success criteria.
- Progress measurements.

```text
Defined Problem
      ↓
Clear Objective
      ↓
Success Metrics
      ↓
Measure Progress
      ↓
Evaluate Solution
```

This makes it easier to determine whether the chosen solution has actually solved the problem.

---

# 4. The One-Sentence Problem Test

### Pro Tip 💡

> **If you cannot explain the problem in one sentence, the problem is probably not clear enough yet.**

Try explaining the problem to someone **outside the project**.

If they can understand:

- What is wrong.
- Why it matters.
- What needs to be addressed.

then the problem statement is probably clear enough.

This technique helps:

- Remove unnecessary jargon.
- Clarify your thinking.
- Identify ambiguity.
- Create shared understanding.
- Improve communication across different roles.

---

# 5. Example: Poor vs. Clear Problem Definition

### Poor Problem Definition

> "The project is delayed."

This describes the situation but does not explain why it is happening.

### Better

> "The testing phase is taking too long."

This identifies a specific symptom but may still not identify the root cause.

### Clear Problem Definition

> "The project is experiencing repeated development and testing delays because the initial requirements were incomplete and unclear."

This identifies the **problem and its underlying cause**.

---

# 6. Problem Definition Checklist

Before moving to the next problem-solving step, ask:

- [ ] Is the problem clearly stated?
- [ ] Are we solving the actual problem rather than a symptom?
- [ ] Have we investigated the root cause?
- [ ] Does everyone involved understand the problem in the same way?
- [ ] Can the problem be explained in one sentence?
- [ ] Can we identify measurable success criteria?
- [ ] Have assumptions and ambiguities been removed?

---

# 7. Key Takeaways

1. **Define the problem before attempting to solve it.**
2. A visible issue may be a **symptom rather than the root cause**.
3. Clear problem definitions provide **direction and team alignment**.
4. They reduce **wasted time, resources, and rework**.
5. They encourage **root-cause analysis**.
6. They improve **communication and collaboration**.
7. They create a foundation for **measurable success**.
8. If you cannot explain the problem in **one sentence**, clarify it further.

## Core Principle

> **Solve the right problem, not merely the most visible problem.**
